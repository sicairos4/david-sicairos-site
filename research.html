What belongs here

Public-records responses, official filings and supporting documents can help explain what an agency said, when an action occurred and which questions remain open. A document is a source, not a conclusion by itself.

The collection is being built. Approved entries will become available as they are added. An empty result does not establish that a document does not exist, that an agency failed to respond or that a filing was not submitted.

Three ways to explore

  1. Public Files is the document catalog. Search indexed titles, agencies, descriptions and keywords, then follow the document link. Search is not a full-text scan of every PDF.
  2. Master Tracker provides agency and subject context and next actions. Its editorial notes are not the source documents themselves.
  3. 209 Signal and 209 Intelligence provide connected reporting and analysis. Read explanations alongside the sources they cite.

Reading a record with care

Look at the issuer, document date, reporting period and retrieval date. A later amendment may change a filing without erasing the earlier version. A response may cover only part of a request. Missing pages or attachments can change what a record means.

Keep the document's wording separate from interpretation. An unanswered question, a disclosed interest or an incomplete production is not by itself a finding of wrongdoing.

Public documents, private working material

The public catalog is intended for approved release copies. Original intake, private correspondence and working notes are kept separate from public entries. A release copy may differ from its original because of redaction; the catalog identifies its checksum as the original-source checksum.

Your private Workstation plans and reflections are a separate experience. Using the planner is not a request to publish your writing.

Request a record or suggest a correction

Use the CPRA Playbook for the site's request guidance. For a catalog correction, email sicairos4@gmail.com with the public page link, document ID if available and a short description of the issue. Do not send passwords, sensitive identifiers or confidential documents with a routine correction request.
