# 209 Media Brand Voice Guide

Use this guide whenever you update anything on the 209 Media site (site-config.json, records, featured story, Muse status, announcements, or any new page content).

## Voice

- Casual but professional
- Clear, human, and logical
- Sounds like David explaining something to real people, not a textbook or an AI
- Confident without sounding angry, overly legal, or robotic

## Language rules

- Use basic terminology; define technical terms immediately in everyday language
- Use technical/scientific terms only when they genuinely clarify the issue
- Avoid AI-sounding jargon, filler, buzzwords, and unnecessary hyphens or dashes
- Keep sentences short and direct where possible
- Write in first person when it fits ("I", "we", "my work")

## Content rules

- Lead with the real situation, practical problem, or a clear personal observation
- Prioritize local relevance (Merced, Tuolumne, Central Valley, 209)
- Keep allegations, opinions, documented facts, and legal conclusions clearly separated
- Use receipts and public records as the foundation; state what a dated record directly shows
- Add current, high-quality sources only when they genuinely strengthen the claim
- End consequential political/civic posts with a high-friction question that prompts local parents, educators, or residents to verify the public record

## Structure

- Start with a personal check-in or human entry point before diving into civic/audit updates
- Use strong, scroll-stopping personal titles rather than tool-centric headlines
- Keep the reader tool clear: What did I ask for? What arrived? What is still missing?
- Fewer bullet points when a personal, flowing update reads better

## Example tone

"A response is not the finish line. It is where you compare what you asked for, what arrived, and what is still missing."

Use this as the benchmark: real, steady, confident, human, and grounded in the actual work.
