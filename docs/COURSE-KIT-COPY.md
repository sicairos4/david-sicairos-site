## Course kit 1: Build Your Intentional Day

### Proposed landing-page description

Turn good intentions into a daily structure you can use. Build a small morning routine, choose what matters today and make room for work, rest and adjustment. Through guided examples and practical exercises, develop a routine, a realistic day plan and a reflection you can return to.

Created by David Sicairos, drawing on academic training in kinesiology, psychology and educational counseling and a teaching focus on movement, motivation and practical learning.

### Intended learner and deliverable

For people who want a clearer starting point without another complicated productivity system. The intended final artifact is one personal routine/day/reflection plan, not a health score or a clinically evaluated treatment program.

### Proposed four-lesson outline

1. Choose a meaningful priority and a small starting action.
2. Build a routine around recognizable cues and actual constraints.
3. Give the day room for focused work, responsibilities and rest.
4. Reflect on what helped and decide what to adjust next.

### Practical continuation

Use the existing morning/daily worksheet or deliberately enter the chosen action in Workstation. Opening a tool must not imply that private workbook answers were uploaded or saved.

## Course kit 2: From Personal Brand to First Offer

### Proposed landing-page description

Turn one useful skill into a clear first offer. Identify who you help, explain the problem you want to address and create a small sample of the work. Develop a brand statement, a focused offer, a sample deliverable and a simple publishing plan you can use to ask for feedback.

David Sicairos brings an educator's approach to the sequence: clarify the outcome, show an example, practice the work and review what the learner produced. The course should help you make and explain an offer, not promise revenue or automated business success.

### Intended learner and deliverable

For independent builders who have skills or ideas but need a concrete first version. The intended final artifact is an audience/brand statement, one bounded offer, a usable sample and a short plan for communicating it. Brand-identity questions alone would not fulfill this kit's First Offer promise.

### Proposed four-lesson outline

1. Name an audience, a useful problem and a clear brand statement.
2. Define one bounded offer and what it does and does not include.
3. Build a small sample deliverable instead of only naming a product.
4. Write a simple publishing/feedback plan and decide what to learn from responses.

### Practical continuation

Use the existing business generator and brand/product launch guides, then adapt a draft in Creator if useful. A copied caption, saved draft or opened share sheet is not evidence that an offer was published or sold.

## Course kit 3: Public Records to Clear Evidence

### Proposed landing-page description

Turn a public-interest question into an organized, source-supported brief. Define a neutral research question, draft a focused records request and keep a provenance log as material arrives. Practice separating what a document directly shows from your interpretation, missing information and the next useful question.

Created within the 209 Audit learning path, with an emphasis on clear instruction, source organization and responsible communication. This is an educational research workflow, not legal representation or a claim that a missing document proves wrongdoing.

### Intended learner and deliverable

For people who want to follow a public issue without losing track of sources or overstating conclusions. The intended final artifact is a research question, request draft, provenance log and brief with traceable citations and an explicit uncertainty section.

### Proposed four-lesson outline

1. Write a neutral question and identify the likely record holder.
2. Draft a focused request for existing records with a useful scope.
3. Record source, dates, versions, amendments and page references.
4. Write a brief that distinguishes record wording, interpretation, gaps and next actions.

### Practical continuation

Use the CPRA Playbook, public research guide and reviewed Public Files catalog. Private archive work remains separate from public release. Any sending, uploading or publishing action needs its own deliberate authorization and appropriate review.
